About this trial

GenOMICC (Genetics Of Mortality In Critical Care) is an international, multicentre observational genetic study. It recruits critically ill patients requiring continuous cardiovascular or respiratory monitoring, or any organ support (e.g. HFNO, CPAP, NIV, invasive ventilation, vasopressors/inotropes, ECMO, or renal replacement for acute disease), whose primary diagnosis is a confirmed or suspected infection, or one of a defined list of non-infectious syndromes (pancreatitis; burns >20% BSA; haemophagocytic syndrome; Still’s disease; heat stroke; radiation poisoning; suspected reactions to therapeutic/cell/gene therapies; emerging critical illness syndromes; and, in children, preterm birth <32 weeks or acute seronegative hepatitis). Participation requires only a single DNA sample — a blood sample of 4mls or less, or saliva if blood isn’t possible — with no other study intervention. Bone marrow transplant recipients are the only exclusion.

GenOMICC has recruited continuously since 2015 and is now the largest consented critical care genetics study in the world, spanning 13 countries; during COVID-19 its genetic findings directly identified baricitinib as an effective treatment for critical COVID-19. A sub-study, GenOMICC Acute, extends recruitment with deeper biological sampling at a limited number of sites (patients >20kg, samples obtainable within 48 hours of admission) — check locally whether this site participates.

Screening

In brief: any patient requiring continuous cardiovascular/respiratory monitoring or any organ support, with a primary diagnosis of infection (of any kind) or one of the listed non-infectious syndromes, is eligible unless they are a bone marrow transplant recipient. Consent/assent follows the usual pathway for patients who lack capacity (consultee in England/Wales/NI, relative in Scotland). Full inclusion/exclusion criteria and consent procedures are set out in Protocol v5 (23 Oct 2024) — confirm against the current local site file before screening or consenting a patient.
