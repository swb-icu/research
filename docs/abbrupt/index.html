About this trial

ABBRUPT (Amiodarone vs Beta Blockade for new-onset atrial fibRillation in icU – a Pragmatic sTudy) is a multicentre, open-label, randomised controlled trial comparing amiodarone with beta-blockade for new-onset atrial fibrillation (NOAF) in critically ill adults. Around 10% of ICU patients develop NOAF, which is associated with a higher risk of stroke, myocardial infarction, and longer hospital stay — but there is no clear evidence on which treatment gives the best outcome, so both drugs remain in routine use.

Eligible patients (age ≥16) are randomised to amiodarone or a beta-blocker (choice of agent and dose left to the treating clinician), continued until sinus rhythm has been maintained for 24 hours. The primary outcome is 90-day mortality. The trial aims to recruit 2,560 participants (1,280 per arm) across NHS sites in England, Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland. Recruitment opened May 2024; the ISRCTN registry shows a final enrolment date of 30 June 2026 and status “no longer recruiting” (as at 15 Jun 2026), while BCTU’s own trial page still describes ABBRUPT as open to recruitment — confirm current recruitment status directly with BCTU or your local PI before assuming the trial is closed locally. Study completion (end of 90-day follow-up and analysis) is expected 31 December 2026.

Screening

There is no standalone eligibility tool for ABBRUPT yet. Key inclusion criteria: adult patient in an area of higher care able to deliver the interventions, new-onset AF (or new episode of AF) during the acute illness with a clinical indication to treat, and usual electrolyte (potassium/magnesium) management per site practice. Key exclusions include prior anticoagulant/antiarrhythmic use for AF, contraindicated concomitant medication, serum potassium <4 mmol/L, cardiac surgery during the current admission, thyrotoxicosis, planned withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment within 24 hours, and known pregnancy/breastfeeding. Confirm current criteria against the approved protocol before screening — the inclusion/exclusion list was amended in 2025.
